How it works

Four steps.

STEP 01

Free Consult

Twenty minutes, ideally with both the parent and student. We’ll discuss your student’s goals and difficulties and chart a path forward. I’ll elaborate on my teaching style and what resources we’ll use, and you can ask any questions about the process.

STEP 02

Diagnostic

Before our first session, a full-length practice test or a problem set in the course. It helps us identify problem areas and hone in on what needs the most attention.

STEP 03

Weekly sessions

One- to two-hour meetings, over video with a shared whiteboard. Homework between sessions is short and specific. We’ll often work with my library of worksheets and practice tests, which cover general practice and specific skill builders.

STEP 04

Progress notes

After each session I send a short recap to the parent. This usually includes progress updates, what we focused on that day, and updated goals for the following week.

Questions

Before you book.

Is remote tutoring as good as in person?

For many students, it is often better. A shared digital whiteboard keeps everything neatly organized so students can easily look back and find specific review topics and problems. Flexible scheduling allows students to easily incorporate sessions into their busy, often variable calendars.

How many sessions will we need?

For a course grade, usually weekly through the term. For test prep, most families start about two months out from the exam date. We'll design an individualized plan dependent on your student's exact needs.

What do you need from us?

A quiet room and a laptop. A tablet with a stylus is helpful for using a collaborative whiteboard, but it is not required.

Do you guarantee a score?

I’d love to, but everything depends on individual circumstance. We’ll carefully look together at where your student stands and what improvements we can aim for in the timeframe available. If you have specific point goals, we can outline what kind of program would be best to achieve them. I don’t make promises I can’t deliver on, and all of the students I’ve taught so far have been pleased with their outcomes.

Which time zones do you cover?

All U.S. time zones, as well as others depending on the circumstance.

Do you work with students who are already doing well?

Often. Some of the best work happens with a student sitting on a 1450 who wants a 1550, or one who has run out of math at their school and wants to go further.
